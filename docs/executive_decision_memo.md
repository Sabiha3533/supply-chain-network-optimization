# EXECUTIVE DECISION MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Director of Operations & Supply Chain Leadership  
**FROM:** Data & Systems Analyst  
**SUBJECT:** Optimal Multi-Tier Distribution Routing & Annual Raw Material Procurement Strategy  

---

### 1. Executive Recommendation
To minimize total logistics and raw material expenditures while meeting all technical, infrastructure, and labor-continuity requirements, we recommend adopting a **hybrid direct-and-depot distribution plan ($541,000 total freight cost)** alongside a **blended 5-vendor procurement portfolio ($73,267,500 total material spend; $59.81/ton average)**.

---

### 2. Key Findings: Distribution Network Optimization
* **Total Cost & Efficiency:** All **430,000 tons** of site demand can be fulfilled at **$541,000** ($1.26/ton average) by combining direct hub-to-site shipments with high-efficiency depot transfers.
* **Direct vs. Depot Routing:** Hub Alpha should ship **140,000 tons directly** to Sites S1 (`100k`) and S6 (`40k`), avoiding unnecessary transfer handling. Hub Beta should route **290,000 tons** through Depot Central (`100k`), Depot East (`110k`), and Depot South (`80k`).
* **Facility Bottlenecks:** **Depot Central** and **Depot South** operate at **100% throughput capacity**, whereas Depot North carries zero volume under current lane rates. Any future demand growth near Sites S2, S3, or S4 will require either expanding Depot Central/South capacity or renegotiating lane rates through Depot North.

---

### 3. Key Findings: Vendor Procurement & Blending Strategy
* **Why Blending Outperforms Strict Filtering:** A naive policy of rejecting suppliers below the **19.0% volatility** specification would eliminate our three lowest-cost bidders. By blending lower-volatility coal (**Vendor B at 600k tons** and **Vendor A at 55k tons**) with high-volatility coal (**Vendor G at 450k tons**, **Vendor E at 100k tons**, and **Vendor D at 20k tons**), we achieve an exact **19.00% technical blend** while saving millions over high-volatility-only sourcing.
* **Infrastructure Bottleneck (Truck Receiving):** Truck deliveries hit the maximum **720,000-ton terminal cap**, while Rail retains **145,000 tons of unused capacity**. Expanding truck unloading throughput would allow increased purchasing from lower-cost truck suppliers.
* **Labor Continuity Buffer:** **55.10%** of total volume is awarded to union-certified suppliers, comfortably exceeding our **50.0% risk-mitigation policy** without sacrificing cost efficiency.
